How to spot a rogue “quick sale” company

The fast-sale industry has genuine, well-funded buyers — and it has bad actors. Protect yourself by checking for these red flags before you sign anything:

  • Last-minute price drops. The classic scam: agree a price, wait until you're committed and desperate, then cut the offer days before exchange. Ask directly: “Is your written offer the completion price?” and get it in writing.
  • Tie-in contracts. Never sign an agreement that stops you selling to anyone else for months. Reputable buyers don't need to trap you.
  • Upfront fees. A genuine cash buyer never charges you for a valuation, a survey or an “administration fee”.
  • No proof of funds. A real cash buyer can show bank statements proving they hold the money. Brokers who merely “find a buyer” cannot — and your sale depends on someone you've never met.
  • No ombudsman membership. Look for membership of The Property Ombudsman (TPO) and the National Association of Property Buyers (NAPB), which give you an independent complaints route.

Cash buyer vs estate agent: an honest comparison

A good estate agent will usually achieve a higher headline price. A cash buyer gives you speed and certainty. The right answer depends on your situation:

  • Choose an estate agent if you can wait 4–6 months, the property is in good condition, and price matters more than anything else.
  • Choose a cash buyer if you're on a deadline (relocation, repossession, probate, broken chain), the property is hard to mortgage or in poor condition, or the stress of viewings and chains outweighs the price difference.

Do the real maths: from the agent's likely price, subtract commission (1–3% + VAT), your legal fees, and every month of mortgage interest, council tax, insurance and utilities while you wait — then weigh in the ~1 in 3 chance the sale falls through and the process restarts.

Selling an inherited or probate property

You generally need a Grant of Probate (or Letters of Administration) before you can complete a sale, which takes some months to obtain. But you don't have to wait to get organised: we can value the property, make a formal offer, and have solicitors fully prepared so completion happens days after probate is granted — instead of the process only starting then. We buy inherited properties as they stand: no clearing, no cleaning, no renovations needed.

Facing repossession: your options

If you've fallen behind on your mortgage, act early — options shrink as the court date approaches. Talk to your lender (they must consider alternatives), get free advice from Shelter, StepChange or Citizens Advice, and if selling is the right move, a completed cash sale can repay the arrears, protect your remaining equity, and avoid a forced sale at auction. Courts will often pause proceedings when a genuine sale is progressing — evidence of an agreed cash sale with solicitors instructed carries real weight.
